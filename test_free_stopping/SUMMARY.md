# Test-free stopping under grokking: where the project stands

*Summary for the research supervisor, 2026-11-16. It condenses RAMPUP, PLAN, DESIGN,
METRICS, THEORY, AGOP.md, results/phase1–4 and the three metric reports
([report_AGOP](report_AGOP/REPORT.md), [report_N1](report_N1/REPORT.md),
[report_R1](report_R1/REPORT.md)). Every number below is taken from those reports'
`tables/summary.csv` or `tables/numbers.txt`, or was recomputed from the HF outputs for
this summary (marked ‡). Section 7 answers the questions we expect.*

---

## 0. The answer so far, in five sentences

1. We asked whether a network can be stopped **without test data and without being fooled
   by grokking**. We tested three theory-backed signals: the learned features (F2, via the
   AGOP), the weight norm (N1) and the effective rank of the hidden activations (R1). Each
   was compared, on held-out runs, with stopping at a tuned fixed fraction of the budget.
2. **On modular arithmetic, all three give adaptive, grokking-safe stops.** Their tuned
   rules never or almost never stop a grokking run before it generalizes, and they cut the
   cost of training to the end by roughly 40–60%. The exception is R1 on new seeds: it
   cuts the cost by only 20% and cuts off 3 of 30 grokkers.
3. **On average they only tie the tuned fixed fraction**, because most runs finish early.
   The fixed fraction's failures are concentrated on a minority of late grokkers, and its
   savings on everything else pay for them.
4. **Their real advantage is robustness to a change of task.**
   - A fraction tuned on x + y lands in the middle of the grokking jump for x × y and
     x ÷ y, and cuts off 16 of 31 grokkers.
   - The signals cut off none.
   - R1 is the only one clearly better than that fraction on average. A 50% or 70% stop,
     which nothing in the tuning data pointed to, would tie it.
5. **On MNIST, every "has it stopped changing?" signal is late by construction.** The
   network keeps compressing (norm, rank and features) long after test accuracy has
   settled, so N1, R1 and F2 can at best tie the fixed fraction there. F2 loses to it on
   both MNIST tests, and N1 loses on the 8-layer network.

## 1. The question

- **Goal:** a stopping rule that is
  - **causal:** the decision at step t uses only data up to t;
  - **test-free:** it uses training data, weights, gradients, recorded telemetry and
    hyperparameters, including γ;
  - **grokking-robust:** it does not stop on the plateau where training accuracy is 100%
    and test accuracy has not moved yet.
- **Why it is hard:** on that plateau the training loss is flat.
  - So the standard test-free rules are expected to fire there: training-loss patience
    (B1), Mahsereci's gradient-evidence test (B2) and Label Wave (B4).
  - The MNIST pilot confirmed this for B4: it stopped all 3 grokkers at about 2k steps.
  - Validation patience (B6) also tends to stop there, because validation loss often rises
    during memorization.
- **What a candidate must therefore measure:** something that **keeps moving during the
  plateau and goes quiet only once the network has settled**: the norm, the features, the
  representation, or the regularized gradient.

## 2. What was built (Phases 0–3)

### Corpus

- **Size:** 1,664 training runs, 78.6 GB on HF (`advay333/tfs`, public), 12.7 GPU-hours
  on Molab.
- **Architecture:** plain MLP with no normalization.
- **Applications:**

  | Application | Network | Loss | Training set |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Modular arithmetic, p = 97: x+y, x×y, x÷y | 3 × 512, ReLU | cross-entropy, full batch | — |
  | MNIST | 3 × 200, or 8 × 400 | MSE (Omnigrok's setup) | 1k or 4k images |
  | Teacher–student | 5-100-100-5, tanh | MSE, full batch | — |

- **Optimizers:** AdamW, Adam+L2, SGD+momentum, SGD.
- **Grid:**
  - weight decay γ: 4 levels per (application, optimizer), always including 0;
  - init scale α: 3 levels;
  - training size: 3 levels;
  - 4 seeds.
- **Learning rates:** chosen by a stability scan (Phase 2a).
- **γ levels:** placed so that grok times spread over about 2–3 decades. Gate G2 passed.

### Recording and reproducibility

- **Recording:** dense telemetry every 50 steps (per-layer norm, gradient and update
  statistics, train predictions), dense summaries every 500 steps (singular values,
  activation-covariance spectra, gradient-noise statistics), and full keyframes every 10k
  steps.
- **Anything else** is regenerated by **bit-exact deterministic replay**.
- **Every run was replay-checked at L0** (bit-identical, zero error), and 84 randomly
  audited runs were replayed end to end at L0. Runs with identical configurations
  reproduce across Molab sessions to every digit.

### Safeguards in code

- **Causality:** signals see only a forward-only observation stream. A test checks that
  truncating a run leaves every earlier signal value unchanged.
- **Test quarantine:** only the evaluation code can read test accuracy. A test scans
  signal, rule and replay code for any access.

### The corpus behaves as the literature predicts

- t_ε ∝ 1/γ.
- Larger α groks later.
- γ = 0 never generalizes on modular.
- The Omnigrok MNIST and teacher–student setups are reproduced.
- Deep Grokking's two-stage generalization did *not* reproduce and was dropped as a target.
- All the traps a rule must survive are present: grokking plateau, joint plateau, and
  late collapse of both types (METRICS §5).

## 3. How every rule is evaluated (identical in all three studies)

### Score (lower is better)

> **cost = (best test acc − test acc at stop) + λ · max(0, stop − t_ε) / T**

- **t_ε** is the first step within 1 point of the run's best test accuracy, and T is the
  budget. A rule that never fires is scored at T.
- **λ = 0.1 is the headline:** wasting the whole budget costs as much as losing 10 points.
  λ = 0.03 and 0.3 are also reported, and every rule is re-tuned at each λ.
- **Why the λ term exists:**
  - Without it, "never stop" is nearly unbeatable: the median regret at the horizon is
    0.002, because most runs keep their best accuracy to the end.
  - Dividing by T makes tasks with different horizons comparable.

### Candidates in every study

| Candidate | Role |
|---|---|
| Never stop | the default |
| Fixed fraction of the budget (2–100%, 10 options) | the strong, simple baseline |
| The metric's variants | each with tens to hundreds of settings |
| The metric's **tuned pick** | the best setting across all its variants on the tuning runs, chosen by a rule fixed in advance |
| Perfect stop at t_ε | the floor, about 0.006–0.010 |

### Tuning and held-out testing

Each candidate's setting is chosen on tuning runs and scored once on held-out runs:

| Test | Tune on | Score on | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, new seeds | seeds 0–1 | seeds 2–3 of the same settings | reproducibility |
| B, modular | x + y | x × y and x ÷ y | a new task |
| B, MNIST | 3-layer network | 8-layer network on 4× more data | a new architecture |
| B, teacher–student | N = 50, 100 | N = 400 | more data |

### Uncertainty

- **Method:** a 95% percentile cluster bootstrap over hyperparameter *settings*, with
  2,000 resamples and seed 0. Seeds of one setting are not independent, so the setting is
  the unit.
- **Comparisons are paired:** the bootstrap is over the per-run difference between two
  rules on the same runs.
- **Verdict:** **better** or **worse** only when the paired interval excludes 0;
  otherwise a **tie**.
- **What the interval covers:** which settings were held out.
- **What it does not cover:** tuning luck. Figures 11 and 12 of the reports show the gap
  between tuning and held-out scores instead.

### Run selection

- **Which runs:** 224 runs for F2 (modular 40 settings, MNIST 16). Study A used the same
  224 plus 96 teacher–student runs (24 settings), 320 in all.
- **How they were chosen:** whole settings with all four seeds, stratified so that every
  outcome type seen on seed 0 appears.

## 4. The three metrics: definitions and theory

| | F2: AGOP feature settling | N1: weight norm | R1: effective rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity | M = G^{1/2}, G = (1/n)ΣJᵀJ (input AGOP over the training set), normalized; the **angle** between feature states | ‖w‖ over all layers, from telemetry | erank = exp(entropy of the normalized eigenvalues of each hidden layer's centered activation covariance), from summaries |
| Theory | Neural feature ansatz (Radhakrishnan et al.); AGOP alignment rises through grokking (Mallinar et al.) | Omnigrok LU mechanism: decay shrinks the norm into the Goldilocks zone; the norm levels off when the network generalizes | Low-rank bias of weight decay and SGD (Galanti et al.); rank falls with generalization (Deep Grokking); Roy & Vetterli's definition |
| Rule families | A: Banach a posteriori bound θq/(1−q) (extrapolation); B: angle over a lag f·t; C: angle over a lag c·T; D: MSD exponent (drift vs jitter); E: Geweke early-vs-late Z | A: \|Δlog‖w‖\| ≤ ε ("stopped changing"); B: Δlog‖w‖ ≥ −ε ("stopped shrinking"). Optionally in units of the pure-decay rate lr·γ·lag | A: \|Δlog erank\| ≤ ε; B: the same, but only after the mean rank fell 5% below its running maximum (guard against the early rise). Layers: mean, last or all |
| Settings | 954 | 140 | 210 |
| Data needed | replay (Jacobians at 223 eval points per run) | none extra | none extra |
| Checked at | 223 points (150 log-spaced, 150 uniform, merged) | every 500 steps | every 500 steps |

**Unit-free design.**
- Every rule works on log quantities or angles, so the scale of the weights, the width and
  the init scale drop out.
- Lags are either a share of the steps so far (f·t, which needs no budget and is
  invariant to the learning rate) or a share of the budget (c·T).

**Theory review (THEORY.md).** F2's remaining-angle bound is the classical Banach *a
posteriori* contraction estimate on the sphere of normalized feature matrices. N1 is the
unnormalized form of ρ\*, Yaida's fluctuation–dissipation stationarity measure. R1 is
optimizer-agnostic.

## 6. Why: the mechanisms behind the results

Each statement is a measured fact from the reports, not a conjecture.

1. **Most runs finish early.**
   - Median t_ε is about 2–3% of the budget for fast learners, about 38–40% for grokkers on
     modular and MNIST, and 17% for teacher–student grokkers.
   - Any stop in the middle of the budget therefore removes most of the waste. That is
     exactly what the fixed fraction does, and why it is hard to beat on average.
2. **On modular, everything settles with the jump:**
   - the norm: at 3× t_ε it is at 0.89 of its value at t_ε;
   - the rank: 0.46 at 3× t_ε, and flat by the end;
   - the features: alignment with the final features is already 0.80 at t_ε.
   A strict "stopped changing" threshold therefore cannot fire before the jump, and fires
   soon after it. That is the source of the safety.
3. **Stopping a modular grokker early costs about 100 points; on MNIST and teacher–student
   it costs a few.** So one modular cut-off outweighs a lot of saved budget, while on
   MNIST early stops are cheap. That is why the fixed fraction wins on MNIST even though
   it cuts off 5–7 of 10 grokkers.
4. **On MNIST, the dynamics continue long after learning.** After t_ε:
   - by the end of training, the norm falls to 0.53 (3-layer) and 0.78 (8-layer) of its
     value at t_ε;
   - the AGOP's effective rank falls to 0.27;
   - the activation rank falls to 0.54 and 0.81 of its value at t_ε by 3× t_ε;
   - alignment with the final features at t_ε is only 0.44 and 0.27.

   Test accuracy does not change meanwhile. **Even an oracle that knows the final features
   does no better than never stopping on MNIST** (best threshold in hindsight 0.082 and
   0.091, against 0.034 and 0.039 for the fixed fraction). So the F2 failure on MNIST is
   a property of the task, not of the estimator.
5. **Extrapolating the remaining motion is unsafe (F2-A).** The Banach bound assumes the
   contraction holds for all future steps. At the hand-off from memorization to the
   plateau, the speed of the features drops sharply and then stays flat for a long time
   (two timescales). So the bound says "almost done" on the plateau:
   - in the 61-run pilot it fell below 0.1 rad before t_ε in 42 of 54 runs, including
     24 of 28 grokkers;
   - at the stop it underestimated the true remaining angle in 95% of cases;
   - its within-run rank correlation with the truth is 0.37 (modular), −0.01 and −0.17
     (MNIST).

   The plain angle over a window (F2-C) tracks the truth well within runs: 0.79 (modular)
   and 0.81 (MNIST 3-layer). It is weak on the 8-layer network (0.20).
6. **Norm-based rules need weight decay.** All 12 early stops of N1 on the 8-layer network
   are γ = 0 runs: the norm is flat from the start, so the rule fires at step 2,000, the
   earliest it can, while t_ε is between 11k and 90k. The same happens on teacher–student
   at N = 400.
7. **"Stopped shrinking" is the wrong reading.** The norm often *grows* during
   memorization (the median modular run goes from 39 to 57 by t_ε), so N1-B fires on the
   plateau and cuts off 7 grokkers in each test.
8. **The last layer's rank can collapse before the jump.** On modular it often falls
   several-fold at the end of memorization and then sits flat on the plateau. R1's
   last-layer rule cut off 3 of 30 grokkers this way on new seeds. On MNIST the rank first
   *rises* (in 75% and 90% of runs), so R1 needs the guard: without it, it is worse than
   the fixed fraction there.
9. **Teacher–student carries no feature or rank signal.**
   - The inputs are 5-dimensional, so the input AGOP is 5 × 5 and turns only 0.012 rad
     over a whole run.
   - The hidden layers use about 5 directions from start to finish (median erank 5.6,
     5.2 and 5.1).
   - Both rules are close to "never stop" there and tie by default.
10. **The fixed fraction is knife-edge on grokking tasks.** The two studies check rules on
    slightly different steps: "stop at 40%" lands at step 41,600 in the F2 study and at
    40,000 in study A.
    - It cuts off the same 16 of 31 new-operation grokkers in both.
    - Yet it scores 0.090 in one and 0.126 in the other. The difference is how much
      accuracy those runs had gained by the stop.
    - A rule whose score moves by 0.036 with a 1.6%-of-budget shift is fragile under any
      change of task.

## 7. Questions a supervisor is likely to ask

**Q1. Isn't a tuned fixed fraction an unfair baseline? It is tuned too.**
It is tuned on exactly the same runs, with the same procedure and the same held-out
scoring, so the comparison is like for like. It is the right null model: "you knew roughly
how long these runs take". A test-free signal is only worth using if it beats that, or
matches it without needing the knowledge. On a new task the knowledge fails (x+y → x×y,
x÷y), which is where the signals win on safety.

**Q2. Your rules use the budget T as well, don't they?**
Partly.
- The best N1 and R1 rules use a lag of 2–10% of T as their window length. F2-C does the
  same.
- The stop time itself is set by the signal, not by T.
- The budget-free versions (f·t lags; F2-B) tie the fixed fraction on modular but never
  stop on almost half the runs, and are worse on MNIST (§5). This is an honest cost, and
  it should be stated.

**Q3. Is "tie" just low statistical power?**
Partly, yes, and we never claim "equal".
- There are 16–40 independent settings per test, so the paired intervals on modular are
  about ±0.04–0.07 wide.
- A real advantage of 0.02–0.03 would not show as "better".
- The report language is "not clearly better", backed by the per-outcome breakdown in
  `tables/by_outcome.csv`. There, for example, N1 is lower than the fixed fraction on fast
  learners, grokkers and never-learners on modular new seeds. Two stall-then-rise runs
  stopped on the stall (0.45 each) erase the advantage.

**Q4. With 954 + 140 + 210 settings, aren't you fishing?**
The setting is chosen on tuning runs and scored once on held-out runs.
- The "tuned pick" across variants is chosen by a rule fixed in advance. An earlier F2
  draft highlighted the variant that looked best afterwards; this was corrected.
- Tuning optimism is shown (tuning vs held-out score, Figures 11 and 12).
- We do report many verdicts: 3 λ × 4–6 tests × several families per study. At 95%, a few
  "better" or "worse" cells are expected by chance.
- R1's one clear win (modular new operations) is supported beyond the CI by the mechanism
  (the fraction tuned on x+y lands on the jump) and by the "every fraction" check (it ties
  50% and 70%).

**Q5. Seeds of a modular setting are nearly identical. Is Test A meaningful?**
Only as a reproducibility check.
- The median s.d. of best test accuracy across the seeds of a modular setting is 0.000.
- That is why the bootstrap clusters by setting, and why Test B, a new task, a new
  architecture or more data, is the real test of generalization.

**Q6. The runs were chosen stratified by outcome. Are the averages representative?**
No, by design. Rare behaviours are over-represented so that every trap is tested. Averages
are "over a balanced mix", not over the natural distribution of the corpus. Per-outcome
scores are in `tables/by_outcome.csv`.

**Q7. Why not a validation set (B6)?**
- The problem is defined as test-free. B6 is kept only as an oracle reference.
- It is also expected to stop on grokking plateaus, because validation loss often rises
  during memorization.
- B6 has not been scored in the held-out protocol yet; the MNIST pilot used a placeholder
  rule.

**Q8. Are the rules genuinely causal?**
Yes, by construction and by test.
- Signals are computed from a forward-only stream.
- Replay only moves forward from the last keyframe.
- The truncation test (DESIGN §13.5) checks that cutting a run at t leaves every earlier
  value unchanged.
- Test accuracy is read only by the scoring code; a test scans signal, rule and replay
  code for any access.

**Q9. Why not one table comparing F2, N1 and R1 directly?**
Their rules are checked on different step grids:
- F2 is evaluated at 223 log/uniform points, because each point needs a replay.
- N1 and R1 are checked every 500 steps.
- The same "stop at 40%" moves by 1,600 steps between the grids and changes score by
  0.036 (§6, point 10).

Comparing across studies would mostly compare grids. The fix is to re-score N1 and R1 at
F2's 223 points: their data is dense, so no replay or retraining is needed. That is a
planned step.

**Q10. Why does F2 fail if the bound is a theorem?**
- The theorem assumes the contraction holds for all future steps.
- On real runs the motion slows sharply at the end of memorization, then a slow drift
  carries most of the remaining feature change.
- The bound therefore underestimated the true remaining angle at 95% of its stops. The
  hypothesis fails, not the mathematics.
- The non-extrapolating variants (B and C) do not need the hypothesis.

**Q11. Why does N1 fail at γ = 0? Couldn't you just not use it there?**
- Rules may see hyperparameters, so "N1 only when γ > 0" is a legitimate rule.
- THEORY §4.4 proposes a principled fallback: a dimensionless remaining-log-norm bound.
- A minimum-time guard is another option.
- Neither has been tested yet. As it stands the result is: N1 measures weight decay at
  work and means nothing without it.

**Q12. How do the findings relate to Omnigrok and Deep Grokking?**
They confirm both, with refinements.
- **Omnigrok:** on modular the norm shrinks through the jump and levels off after it
  (Omnigrok's mechanism), and t_ε ∝ 1/γ holds in the corpus.
- **Deep Grokking:** the rank does fall with generalization. Two new details:
  - the last layer's rank can collapse before generalization;
  - on MNIST the rank first rises during memorization.
- Deep Grokking's two-stage generalization did not reproduce at depth 8 or 12.

**Q13. What about late collapse (test accuracy peaking, then falling)?**
- Every stationarity signal is late on it by construction, because the damage is done
  before anything settles (METRICS §2, §5).
- Few such runs are in the studies (8 modular, 1 MNIST, 0 teacher–student), so they were
  not tested meaningfully.
- The planned detectors are:
  - B5 (HTSR α < 2 and correlation traps, the literature's anti-grokking detector);
  - a "norm growing" rule (ρ\* < 0) for Type A;
  - a train-accuracy guard for Type B.

**Q14. Was anything decided after looking at the data?**
Yes, and it should be disclosed.
- **The λ term.** It was introduced after the 61-run F2 experiment showed that never
  stopping is nearly unbeatable on plain regret. 36 of those 61 runs are reused in the
  224-run study.
- **The F2 variants B–E** were designed after that experiment showed that extrapolation
  (A) fails.
- **What was fixed in advance:** none of the held-out scores were seen before the
  candidate sets, the tuning rule and the λ values were fixed.

**Q15. Is anything double-counted?**
- One modular setting (x + y, γ = 0, 4 seeds) appears twice, as AdamW and as Adam+L2.
  At γ = 0 both are plain Adam, so the runs are identical. That is 1 duplicate setting
  out of 40 modular ones ‡.
- Effect: negligible, but Phase 5 should count each such pair once.

**Q16. Why MSE on MNIST?**
- It is Omnigrok's MNIST setup.
- Their Appendix E shows that cross-entropy groks less cleanly on MNIST and needs
  α ≈ 100.

## 8. Limitations to state up front

- **Scope:**
  - MLPs only; three tasks; a 100k-step budget (300k for teacher–student).
  - Teacher–student turned out to be uninformative for feature and rank signals
    (5-dimensional inputs).
- **Power:**
  - 16–40 independent settings per test; only 8 for MNIST and teacher–student Test B.
  - The 8-layer MNIST split has only 2 grokkers.
- **Labels:** the outcome labels (grokker, fast, …) come from a provisional rule that
  mislabels slow creeps. The score does not use them; they are used only for counts and
  breakdowns.
- **Decision grids differ** between studies (§7, Q9).
- **The best modular rules use a budget-tied window** (§5).
- **Not yet tested:**
  - B1–B4, B6 and S1/S1b under the held-out protocol;
  - B5 with correlation traps;
  - combined rules, e.g. features settled **and** norm settled, or a signal plus a
    minimum time.

2. **A common decision grid:** re-score N1 and R1 at F2's 223 points, so that all metrics
   share one table (no retraining needed).
3. **Combined and guarded rules** suggested by the mechanisms:
   - a signal plus a γ = 0 fallback or minimum time (fixes N1's failure);
   - the mean layer rather than the last (fixes R1's pre-jump stops);
   - features **and** norm settled (THEORY §4.3).
4. **Decisions still open, for the user:** METRICS §6 (the final metric set, S1's ρ\*
   definition, the γ = 0 fallback, layer aggregation), and whether F2-C stays as a
   candidate on grokking tasks.
5. **Then Phase 5:** calibration and evaluation of the surviving rules on all 1,664 runs.
